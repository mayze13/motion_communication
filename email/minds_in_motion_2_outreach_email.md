## What changed from your draft

- Flyer attachment removed. The site now does that job, and the link sits in bold on its own line so it survives being forwarded.
- Both signup branches spelled out with price, time commitment, dates and what's worn. This is the part a busy person will actually scan.
- Added a specific ask. Your draft implied the firms were being invited to watch, so it was easy to read and then do nothing about. There are now two concrete actions, one cheap and one substantial.
- Added an incentive that costs you very little: a site visit during the September sessions, and a briefing on the findings. Firms respond to access.
- "This is exactly the kind of research worth being close to rather than reading about after the fact" is a good line but it was doing sales work in a paragraph that had no ask attached. I've moved the idea into point 1, where it has something to push against.
- Ethics reference added to the signature. It reads as a real study rather than marketing, which matters when someone is deciding whether to forward a recruitment link to their staff.
- Fixed "AutoDesk" to "Autodesk".

## Notes on specific firms

**ZHA Architects.** They rebranded from Zaha Hadid Architects in 2025, moved to zha.com, and now explicitly disclaim any connection to the Zaha Hadid Foundation. Addressing them by the old name will land badly. Their published routes are press@zha.com and projects@zha.com.

**Google DeepMind.** There's no public inbox and a cold LinkedIn message to a DeepMind researcher has poor odds. There's a much better route sitting inside UCL. Caswell Barry, in UCL Cell and Developmental Biology, was co-first author with Andrea Banino on the 2018 Nature paper on vector-based navigation using grid-like representations in artificial agents, which was the DeepMind grid cell result. His address is published on that paper as caswell.barry@ucl.ac.uk. Ask Hugo whether he knows him, which he very likely does. One warm introduction from Hugo to Caswell is worth more than twenty LinkedIn requests, and Caswell can point you at whoever at DeepMind is currently closest to spatial representation.

If you do want to go direct on LinkedIn anyway, don't search by company alone. Search for people at Google DeepMind whose profiles mention hippocampus, grid cells, spatial memory or cognitive neuroscience, then check whether they've published with anyone in Hugo's orbit. Lead with the shared citation, not the study.

**OpenAI.** This is the weakest fit on your list and I'd deprioritise it. They have no group whose work touches physical spatial cognition in the way DeepMind's does, no public research-partnerships inbox, and no obvious reason to care about a September session at PEARL. If you want an AI lab on the list beyond DeepMind, a robotics or embodied-agents group would be a better use of the slot.

**The general inboxes.** Only four firms on your list publish an address that a human reads: Foster + Partners, ZHA, Cambridge Cognition and g.tec. For everyone else, the contact form is a marketing funnel and your email will not reach anyone who cares. The realistic route is to find one named person per firm on LinkedIn, in workplace research or applied research rather than business development, and construct the address from the known staff format. I've put the formats I could confirm in the CSV.

One more thing worth doing before you send: since these are recruitment sends going out at volume from a UCL mailbox, keep each one individually addressed rather than BCC'd in a block. A firm's spam filter treats a message with one recipient and a real signature very differently from a message with forty.
