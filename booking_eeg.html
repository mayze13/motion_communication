A note on hair and glasses. EEG sensors have to touch the scalp, and the eye-tracker cannot see through glasses. Today's equipment works poorly with thick, braided, locked or covered hair, and there is no good workaround we can offer on the day. Participant information sheet

Minds in Motion · Version 1, 08/08/2026 · Ethics ref EP/2024/020

You are being invited to take part

Before you decide, it is important you understand why the research is being done and what it would involve for you. Please read this page carefully and ask us anything that is not clear.

Who is running the study?

The study is run by Professor Hugo Spiers (UCL Experimental Psychology) and Dr Fiona Zisch (UCL Bartlett School of Architecture), at University College London.

What is the study about?

We are studying how people move through and experience built environments — how the layout of a space affects the way groups of people navigate it. Findings help inform how buildings are designed. We keep this description general on purpose: knowing exactly what we are measuring could change how you behave on the day, which would affect the results.

Why have I been invited?

You registered your interest in taking part in UCL research, or were told about the study by someone who did. We need 32 people for the EEG strand who can attend two sessions at UCL PEARL in September 2026 and who meet the equipment requirements below.

Do I have to take part?

No. Taking part is entirely voluntary. If you decide to take part you can still stop at any point during the session, without giving a reason and without any consequence. You do not have to answer any question you would rather not answer.

What will happen if I take part?

You come to UCL PEARL in Dagenham twice, on two different dates. A researcher greets you, briefs you in person and goes through a consent form with you before anything begins, on both visits.

  • Visit 1 — individual session, about 90 minutes. Just you and the research team. We fit an EEG cap, which records electrical activity at the scalp, and an eye-tracker, which records where you look. You then move through a configured space on your own.
  • Visit 2 — group session, about 90 minutes. The same equipment, but this time you move through the space at the same time as around thirty other participants. Overhead cameras record movement paths across the group.
  • Depending on which dates you choose, the individual visit comes either before or after the group visit.
  • There are breaks, and you can sit down, pause or leave at any time.

What is the EEG cap like?

It is a soft fabric cap holding small sensors against your scalp. It records activity; it does not send anything into you. To get a signal we apply a small amount of water-based conductive gel at each sensor, which washes out and does not stain. Fitting takes around twenty minutes. Most people find it unremarkable; some find the cap slightly snug. You may want to plan to wash your hair afterwards.

Why the requirements about hair and glasses?

The sensors have to make direct contact with the scalp, so hair that cannot be parted — braided, locked, extended, or very thick — prevents a usable recording, as does a head covering worn during the session. The eye-trackers are a special pair of wearable glasses and therefore would be tricky to wear on top of an existing pair of glasses, though contact lenses are completely fine.

We want to be straightforward about this: these are limitations of current EEG and eye-tracking equipment, not judgements about participants, and they exclude some people unfairly — a problem the field is actively working on. The group-only sessions have none of these requirements and are open to far more people. If you are unsure whether the requirements apply to you, please email us rather than ruling yourself out.

What do I need to be able to do?

You need to be able to move around a large indoor space unaided for up to an hour. Because the group session places everyone in the space together, we are not able to accommodate participants who need another person or a mobility aid with them. We check this before you book, so that nobody travels to Dagenham for a session they cannot take part in.

Are there any risks?

The risks are low and everyday: you will be walking and standing for periods, and in the group session you will be in a moderately busy space with other people. The EEG cap and gel are non-invasive; very occasionally people find the gel mildly irritating to the skin, which is why we ask about scalp conditions and adhesive allergies before you book. Nothing invasive or physically demanding is involved, and you can stop at any moment.

Are there any benefits?

You will receive £60 for taking part after your second session. If you complete only the first visit, contact us and we may arrange a partial payment for the time you gave. You will also be contributing to research on how buildings can be designed to work better for the people who use them.

What data do you collect, and what happens to it?

  • Booking details — your name, email address and chosen session, used only to organise the sessions and contact you about them.
  • Questionnaire responses — the demographic and personality questions you answer when booking.
  • Session data — EEG recordings, eye-tracking recordings, and anonymous movement paths, from both visits.

Data is stored on UCL systems. Your responses are separated from your name and anonymised before analysis, and results are only ever reported at group level — never in a way that identifies you. Research data is retained for 10 years.

University College London is the data controller. We process your data on the legal basis of a task carried out in the public interest. You have the right to ask what data we hold about you, to have it corrected, and to ask us to delete it. To do so, or to raise a concern, contact UCL's Data Protection team at data-protection@ucl.ac.uk. Full details are in the UCL General Privacy Notice.

Can I withdraw after taking part?

Yes. You can ask us to remove your data at any point up until it has been anonymised, after which we can no longer identify which data is yours. Email us and we will confirm what has happened to your data.

Who has reviewed this study?

The study has been approved by the UCL Research Ethics Committee, reference EP/2024/020.

What if something goes wrong?

If you have a concern about any aspect of the study, please speak to us first and we will do our best to answer you. If you remain unhappy and wish to complain formally, contact the Chair of the UCL Research Ethics Committee.
