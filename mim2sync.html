The whole setup in one picture

Every device that needs to share a timeline sits on one private Wi-Fi network with no internet. One controller PC records everything and sends the phase markers. Ubisense sits on the separate UCL network and is aligned afterwards.

Bulk EEG and video never cross the network. Wi-Fi carries the eight LSL streams plus tiny clock-offset traffic. The SD card in each amp and the recording on each phone are complete backups regardless of what the network does.

What LSL actually is

There is no LSL machine and no central server. liblsl is a library that software links against. A program creates an outlet and pushes samples in. A program elsewhere creates an inlet and pulls them out. Outlets announce themselves on the local network, then data flows over TCP.

Producers must sit on the hardware. LSL-LiveAmp needs the dongle and Windows drivers, so it runs on every backpack PC. Consumers can sit anywhere, which is why LabRecorder lives on the controller.

Redundant backpack recorders are easy. A LabRecorder on a backpack PC is just another inlet. It discovers the controller's marker outlet on its own, so markers arrive there exactly as they do centrally. Nobody opens the backpack: enable RCS and the controller starts and stops all four over TCP on port 22345. If Wi-Fi drops mid-run you have roughly five minutes to restore it before any samples are actually lost.

What happens when you press the button

At each of the four phase changes, one person presses one key on the controller. Three things happen at once.

Check before you press. Neon throws away any event that arrives while a phone is not recording. The controller polls /api/status on all four phones first and refuses to fire if one is not live.

Do we still need tones and flashing lights?

Almost never, now that the press is the timestamp. The LSL streams get the marker natively, the phone recordings get it by REST, and the EEG on the SD card gets it by cross-correlating one channel against the streamed copy. Nothing is left uncovered except Ubisense, and phases are ten minutes apart, so even a full second of clock skew is under a fifth of a percent of a phase.

Keep one clap at the start and one at the end, in front of all four participants. It costs nothing, it appears in every scene camera and audio track, and it lets you verify the clock assumption rather than trust it.

Getting Ubisense onto the same timeline

Ubisense lives on the UCL network with its own clock and its own log. You do not need to move it. You need the two clocks to agree well enough, which for ten-minute phases is a very low bar.

Make the controller dual-homed: wired Ethernet to the UCL network, Wi-Fi to the private study network. That single change gives you three things. The controller disciplines its clock against the same NTP source as the location engine. It can reach the location engine directly when the bridge eventually gets built. And its UTC-stamped CSV of button presses becomes directly comparable to Ubisense's own timestamped location events.

Alignment then is a table join: match each press time in the CSV against the position log, cut the trajectory into four blocks, done. The start and end clap gives you a second, clock-independent check if anything looks off.

Run sheet

  1. Power the router. Private 5 GHz network only. It does not need internet.
  2. Boot the four backpack PCs. Confirm LSL-LiveAmp connects to each amp by serial number.
  3. Format and arm the SD cards in all four amps. Check the LED before the backpack is closed.
  4. On each phone, open the Companion app, turn on Stream over LSL, confirm it joins the private network.
  5. On the controller, open LabRecorder and confirm nine streams appear: four EEG, four gaze, one markers.
  6. Start the four backpack recorders over RCS, then press record centrally.
  7. Clap once where all four scene cameras can see it. Say the session ID aloud.
  8. Run the session. Press the key at each phase change and say the phase number out loud.
  9. Clap once at the end, before anything is switched off.
  10. Stop recording, pull the four SD cards, upload the phone recordings, export the Ubisense log.

Still open

  1. BLOCKINGUbisense bridge, if you want live position in LSLOffline alignment on UTC works for phase labelling. A bridge is only needed if position must be time-locked to EEG at sub-second resolution. Decide which, then assign an owner.
  2. Amp SD recording while LSL owns the linkBench test whether the LiveAmp will hold its SD recording while LSL-LiveAmp manages the Bluetooth connection, rather than BrainVision Recorder.
  3. Backpack PC under loadConfirm a low-spec mini-PC runs the connector without dropping samples, and that participants can walk naturally wearing it.
  4. Multicast on the private networkLSL discovers streams over multicast. Confirm the router passes it and that phones and PCs share one subnet.
  5. Hat over cap, tag under ampDoes the hat lift electrodes, and does the amp shadow the tag from the ceiling? One bench test answers both.
  6. What a phase actually isIf the maze reconfigures between phases, a boundary is an interval and needs a start and an end press, not one.
  7. Cloud upload and ethics200 Hz gaze needs first-person video of bystanders uploaded to Pupil Cloud. Confirm the consent form covers it.
